# Phase 4 — Motion model

Status: **DRAFT 2026-10-12**, awaiting your review.
Implementation plan: written after approval (docs/phases/phase-4-motion-plan.md).
Last updated: 2026-10-12

## 0. Summary

Train a model that turns music into **player motion**: head and both sabers
over time, plus a per-hand **hit channel** that marks when each hand cuts a
note. Phase 5 turns that output into a map; Phase 4 only has to make the
motion plausible, controllable and well timed.

- **Data**: Tier A BeatLeader replays (K = 3 per difficulty), resampled to
  50 fps on the audio clock, in a world-anchored play-space frame.
  Left-handed replays are recovered by mirroring.
- **Model**: an EDGE-style diffusion transformer (~45M parameters). It
  denoises 12.8 s windows of motion + hits, cross-attends to the Phase 2
  audio features, and is conditioned on difficulty label and target NPS with
  classifier-free guidance. Long songs are generated by overlapping
  windows, where each window's first half is held to the previous one.
- **Compute**: every training run fits one night (≤ 12 h) on the RTX 5080,
  with exact resume, so the PC is free during the day.
- **Evaluation**: hit timing against the Phase 3 baseline, physical
  plausibility, hit/motion agreement, control, diversity and seams, all
  measured against real held-out replays, plus a visual check in a local
  3D viewer.

Nothing here is measured on Phase 4 data yet: the replays are not fetched.
Numbers marked **[verify in pilot]** are checked on the 1,256 accepted pilot
replays already on disk (phase-1-data.md §12) before the full fetch.
Throughput and I/O items are marked **[verify at setup]**.

### Decisions (agreed 2026-10-12)

| # | Decision | Outcome | Rejected alternative |
|---|----------|---------|----------------------|
| D1 | Model output | **Motion + per-hand hit channel** (hit frame + sub-frame offset) | Motion only (Phase 5 guesses hits from swing peaks); motion conditioned on a map (a player simulator, not a generator) |

## 1. Goals and non-goals

**Goals**
1. A generator: audio features + difficulty label + target NPS in, head and
   saber motion plus per-hand hits out, for a whole song.
2. Hit timing that beats the Phase 3 baseline at its own measure (±30 ms
   F against Tier A maps).
3. Motion that is physically plausible and consistent with its own hits,
   by comparison with real held-out replays.
4. Controls that work: generated density follows the requested NPS, and
   difficulty labels order as expected.
5. Section regeneration ("regenerate these 8 bars") falls out of the
   long-song mechanism (§5.6), ready for Phase 6.
6. A viewer to watch generated and real motion against the audio, and a
   report regenerated on each run, like Phases 1–3.

**Non-goals (Phase 4)**
- Lanes, layers, cut directions, dots, bombs, walls: Phase 5 extracts these
  from motion.
- Anything about how the motion feels in VR: Phase 5 playtests.
- Player or mapper style conditioning: Phase 6.
- Tier B replays (D6).
- Uploading generated output anywhere (§12).

## 3. Replays → motion tracks (`kata motion prepare`)

### 3.1 Resampling

Raw frames arrive at ~72–120 Hz with jitter (phase-1-data.md §6.2). Each
accepted replay is resampled onto a **50 fps grid**, `t = i / 50` s, on the
song clock: the same clock as map `time_s` and Phase 2 features (every
second 100 fps audio frame).

- Positions: linear interpolation.
- Rotations: slerp, after flipping quaternion signs so consecutive frames
  are in the same hemisphere.
- **Invalid frames**: any grid frame whose neighbouring raw frames are more
  than 100 ms apart (tracking loss, stalls), and non-monotonic stretches.
  They are kept but flagged; training windows never contain them (§6.2).
- The grid spans the replay's first to last raw frame.

### 3.2 Coordinate frame

- **Axes as the game stores them**: +y up, +z towards the incoming notes,
  +x right, metres (phase-1-data.md §6.2). No conversion to a right-handed
  frame; mirroring (§3.5) is defined in these axes.
- **Centring**: subtract the replay's median head x and z from every
  position. This removes where the player stands in the room.
- **Height**: y stays absolute, because notes arrive at fixed world
  heights. **[verify in pilot]**: regress hand y at hits on
  `height_median`, per note layer. If the slope is clearly non-zero, y is
  normalised by `height_median` instead, and the generator takes a height
  argument (default: the train median).
- **Hands are world-anchored** (D8): saber positions are in the centred
  play-space frame, not relative to the head.
- **Saber offsets** (BSOR section 6) are not applied. **[verify in pilot]**
  with the blade check (§3.5): if the recorded pose does not put the blade
  through the note at cut time, apply the offsets and re-check.

### 3.3 Per-frame vector (31 channels)

| Channels | Count | Content |
|----------|-------|---------|
| `head_pos` | 3 | centred position |
| `head_rot6` | 6 | 6D rotation (first two columns of the rotation matrix; Zhou et al., §15) |
| `left_pos`, `left_rot6` | 9 | left saber |
| `right_pos`, `right_rot6` | 9 | right saber |
| `left_hit`, `right_hit` | 2 | hit pulse (§3.4) |
| `left_off`, `right_off` | 2 | sub-frame offset of the hit, in frames, in [−0.5, 0.5) |

### 3.4 Hit labels (D1, D10)

A **hit** is a good or bad cut (`replay_note_events.event_type`) of a colour
note or chain head, attributed to the saber that made it (`saberType` from
the cut info). Misses and bombs do not count.

- Time: the **map time** `time_s` of the matched note (`matched_note_idx`),
  not the player's cut time (D10).
- Frame: `round(time_s · 50)`; offset: `time_s · 50 − frame`.
- Several notes cut by one hand in the same frame (stacks, towers) merge
  into one hit; the offset is their mean.
- Stored as a binary flag per frame plus the offset. The training target
  turns flags into a Gaussian bump (§5.5).

Expert+ streams are ~125 ms per hit per hand, about 6 frames, so hits by
the same hand never collide at 50 fps.

### 3.5 Left-handed replays and mirroring (D7)

Left-handed replays (5.4 % of the pilot) are downloaded anyway: the flag is
only known from the BSOR. `prepare` re-parses their BSORs from
`raw/replays` (no network) and recovers them:

1. **Notes**: mirror each `noteID` back before alignment: lane `x → 3 − x`,
   colour `0 ↔ 1`, cut direction left ↔ right (2 ↔ 3, 4 ↔ 5, 6 ↔ 7; up,
   down and dot unchanged). Accept with the same gate as Phase 1: ≥ 98 %
   matched, p95 |residual| ≤ 2 ms.
2. **Frames**: the mirror is the reflection `x → −x`. Positions become
   `(−x, y, z)`; quaternions `(qx, qy, qz, qw) → (qx, −qy, −qz, qw)`; left
   and right sabers swap. **[verify in pilot]** whether the game mirrors
   the frames as well as the notes: the transform kept is the one
   (identity, or mirror + swap) under which the blade check passes.
3. **Blade check** (all replays, not only left-handed): at each matched
   cut, the distance from the note's world position to the segment from
   saber origin to blade tip. Blade = 1 m along the saber's local +z
   **[verify in pilot]**. Note world positions follow the game's grid
   (lane centres at x = −0.9, −0.3, 0.3, 0.9 m) **[verify in pilot]**; the
   check compares distributions, so a constant offset only shifts them. A
   replay passes when the median distance is within the pilot's real
   distribution (p95 of accepted right-handed replays).

The same reflection, applied to a right-handed replay together with its
map, is **mirror augmentation**. It is off by default and gets one ablation
(§6.5).

### 3.6 Normalisation

`motion/v1/stats.json` holds per-channel mean and std of the 27 motion
channels over **train only**. They are applied at load time, as Phase 2
does for audio. Hit pulses map to [−1, 1]; offsets are multiplied by 2.

### 3.7 Tables and arrays

- `motion/v1/{score_id}.npy`: (T, 31) fp16, one per accepted replay.
  fp16 keeps positions to ~0.5 mm at 1 m.
- `motion/v1/{score_id}.valid.npy`: (T,) bool.
- `processed/motion_tracks.parquet`: `score_id, hash, characteristic,
  difficulty, split, label, nps, hps, n_frames, n_valid, n_hits_left,
  n_hits_right, mirrored, blade_median_m, status, reject_reason,
  motion_version`.

`nps` is the difficulty's song-level density: notes ÷ (last note time −
first note time), computed by Phase 3's own `kata.validate.rules.nps`, so
it is the quantity the strata use. It is the conditioning value (D2).
`hps` is the same function applied to this replay's hit times (both
hands, unique times). Hits merge stacked notes (§3.4), so `hps < nps` on
maps with stacks; control is measured in hits (§7.2.4).

## 4. Audio conditioning

### 4.1 Features

Per 10 ms audio frame, all Phase 2 arrays: `mel` (128) + `stem_mel` (256)
+ `chroma` (12) + `curves` (12) = **408 values**, normalised with Phase 2's
train-split `stats.json`. Pairs of frames are stacked (816 values) to reach
50 fps.

Added per 50 fps frame: beat phase and bar phase as sin/cos (4 values),
interpolated between `audio_beats` events. These come from the **audio**,
never the map's BPM grid, so training sees exactly what generation sees.

### 4.2 Context

The audio window reaches **2 s past each end** of the motion window (100
extra frames each side; 840 audio frames for a 640-frame window). Players
react to notes about a second before they arrive, so the model needs
look-ahead. Beyond the song's ends the features are padded with the
normalised silence value (mel at −100 dB).

## 5. Model (D5)

### 5.1 Window

**640 frames = 12.8 s** (8 bars at 150 BPM). Fixed in seconds rather than
bars, so batching is simple; tempo reaches the model through the beat
features. Ablation: 320 frames (§6.5).

### 5.2 Audio encoder

Linear 820 → 512, then 4 transformer encoder layers (d = 512, 8 heads,
rotary positions, dropout 0.1). Output: 840 audio tokens.

### 5.3 Denoiser

8 transformer layers, d = 512, 8 heads, rotary positions, dropout 0.1.
Each layer: self-attention over the 640 motion frames, cross-attention to
the audio tokens, feed-forward (4×). Input and output: linear 31 ↔ 512.

**Conditioning** by FiLM in every layer from one vector: diffusion step
(sinusoidal embedding), difficulty label (learned embedding, 5 labels +
"none"), NPS (`log(nps)` through a small MLP, + "none"). About **45M
parameters** in total.

### 5.4 Diffusion

- The network predicts the **clean sample x₀**, not the noise (as EDGE).
  That makes the geometric losses below possible.
- Cosine noise schedule, 1,000 training steps; **DDIM with 50 steps** for
  sampling.
- **Classifier-free guidance**: label and NPS are each replaced by "none"
  with probability 0.1, independently. At sampling,
  `x̂ = x̂_uncond + w · (x̂_cond − x̂_uncond)`, where "uncond" drops both;
  `w` is tuned on val (§7). Audio is never dropped.

### 5.5 Losses

| Term | Definition | Weight |
|------|------------|--------|
| `L_simple` | MSE between predicted and true x₀, all 31 channels; offset channels only at true hit frames | 1 |
| `L_tip` | MSE on saber-tip positions (origin + 1 m along local +z), in metres, computed from the de-normalised prediction | 1 **[tune at setup]** |
| `L_tip_vel` | MSE on frame-to-frame tip velocity | 1 **[tune at setup]** |

Hit targets are a Gaussian bump around each hit frame (σ = 1 frame, peak 1,
mapped to [−1, 1]) instead of a single-frame spike, which a regression loss
would smear. **Decoding**: a hit is a local maximum of the pulse above 0
(the midpoint, i.e. 0.5 before mapping), at `(frame + offset) / 50` s.

### 5.6 Long songs (overlap inpainting)

Windows advance by half a window (320 frames, 6.4 s). The first window is
generated freely. For each later window, at **every** denoising step the
first 320 frames are replaced by the previous window's last 320 frames,
noised to that step's level (EDGE's long-form scheme). The output keeps
each window's second half. Generation is deterministic for a given seed.

The same replacement with an arbitrary mask is **section inpainting**
(regenerate frames a..b, keep the rest), which Phase 6 needs. Phase 4
exposes it but does not evaluate it.

**Cost**: a 3-minute song is ~28 windows × 50 steps × 2 guidance passes
≈ 2,800 forward passes, roughly 30 s per difficulty **[verify at setup]**.

### 5.7 Public interface

```python
kata.motion.generate(audio_sha256: str, label: str | None, nps: float | None,
                     seed: int = 0, guidance: float | None = None,
                     run: str | None = None) -> MotionTrack
```

`MotionTrack` holds `t` (T,), `head`, `left`, `right` as positions (T, 3)
and rotations (T, 3, 3) in the de-normalised play-space frame (§3.2), and
`hits_left`, `hits_right` as sorted arrays of hit times in seconds.
`guidance=None` uses the run's tuned value. `run=None` uses the run marked
final in `runs/motion/final`. Phase 5 consumes only this interface.

## 6. Training

### 6.1 Splits

Inherited from Phase 1 (song groups), unchanged:
- **train**: training and `stats.json`;
- **val**: checkpoint selection, guidance weight, ablations;
- **test**: touched **once**, for acceptance (§8).

### 6.2 Window sampling

Uniform over **songs**, then over that song's difficulties with replays,
then over that difficulty's replays, then a uniform window start such that
all 640 frames are valid. Song-first sampling keeps a song with 5
difficulties × 3 replays from counting 15 times as much as a song with one.
An epoch is defined as 1,000 steps; there is no notion of a full pass.

### 6.3 Optimisation

- Batch 64 windows, bf16 autocast, AdamW (β = 0.9, 0.99, weight decay
  0.01), lr 2e-4, 2k warmup steps, cosine decay to 10 % over the run's
  planned steps.
- **EMA** of the weights (decay 0.999); sampling and evaluation use the EMA
  copy.
- Throughput estimate ~0.5 s per step ⇒ ~80k steps per 12 h night
  **[verify at setup]**.

### 6.4 Runs, checkpoints and resume (D3)

- `runs/motion/{run_id}/config.json` holds every hyperparameter; a run
  refuses to resume with a different config.
- A checkpoint every **30 minutes** and at exit: model, EMA, optimiser,
  scheduler, step, every RNG state and the sampler state.
  `kata motion train --resume {run_id}` continues **exactly** where it
  stopped. A test checks that N steps straight and N/2 + resume + N/2 give
  identical weights.
- `--hours 12` stops the run cleanly at the time limit. The planned step
  count (for the lr schedule) is set at creation and can span nights.
- Every 5k steps: val loss, plus the fast val metrics (§7.1, criteria 1 and
  4 on 50 val difficulties) into `log.jsonl`. The kept checkpoint
  (`best.pt`) is chosen on those metrics, not on loss.

### 6.5 Experiment schedule

| # | Night(s) | Run | Data |
|---|----------|-----|------|
| 0 | evening | **Prototype**: overfit one song (loss → ~0, hits exact); throughput; I/O | pilot replays |
| 1 | 1 | **Pilot run**, base config, 12 h | accepted pilot replays with features (§10) |
| 2 | 1 | **Base run**, 12 h | full Tier A |
| 3 | 2 | **Ablations**, two 6 h runs per night, compared at equal steps on val: window 320 vs. 640; mirror augmentation on vs. off; stems on vs. off (stems are 256 of the 408 audio inputs) | full Tier A |
| 4 | 1–2 | **Final run** with the best configuration, resumed for a second night if val metrics still improve | full Tier A |

About **6 nights** in total. An ablation is adopted only if it improves
criterion 1 or 2 on val without making any other criterion worse.

### 6.6 Data loading

Motion arrays and Phase 2 feature `.npy` files are memory-mapped and read
as random windows. Tier A audio features are ~46 GB, more than RAM (31 GB),
so they stay on E:. **[verify at setup]**: random-window read throughput
from E: with the planned number of `DataLoader` workers on Windows (spawn).
Fallbacks in order: a one-time copy of the train songs' features to the
internal SSD; a single-process loader with a prefetch thread.

## 7. Evaluation

### 7.1 Protocol

For each evaluated difficulty (val during development, test once), two
samples with seeds 0 and 1, using that difficulty's real label and NPS.
Every metric is also computed on the **real held-out replays** of the same
difficulties, so each bar is relative to real players.

The guidance weight `w` is chosen on val from {1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4} by
criterion 1, subject to criteria 2 and 4 passing.

### 7.2 Metrics

1. **Hit timing**: F-measure at ±30 ms between generated hit times (both
   hands, unique times) and the Tier A map's note times for the same song
   and label. Exactly Phase 3's definition and code (`f_measure`,
   `TIMING_TOL_S`), so the baseline's `timing_f` is directly comparable.
2. **Physical plausibility**: distributions of saber-tip speed at hits,
   peak tip speed per swing, wrist roll range per hand, hand–head distance,
   and head displacement over 1 s windows. Compared with real by p50 and
   p95.
3. **Hits agree with motion**: share of hits within ±40 ms of a local
   maximum of that hand's tip speed, with the tip moving at ≥ 1 m/s at the
   hit.
4. **Control**: generated hit rate (`nps()` on the generated hit times)
   against the requested NPS (rank correlation) and against the real
   replays' `hps` for the same difficulty (ratio, same units); and with NPS
   dropped, generated hit rate per label.
5. **Diversity**: mean distance between the two samples' saber-tip
   trajectories (after aligning their hits by dynamic time warping),
   compared with the same distance between real players on the same map.
6. **Seams**: tip-velocity change across each window join, compared with
   the real p99 frame-to-frame change.

### 7.3 Viewer

`kata motion viewer` writes one self-contained HTML page per clip to
`reports/motion-viewer/`:
- a three.js scene: head (with facing direction), red and blue sabers as
  1 m blades, hits flashing on the blade; for real replays, the map's notes
  flying in;
- a file picker for the song's audio, played in sync;
- **blind mode**: two clips of the same song and label, generated and real,
  in random order; you guess which is real before it is revealed.

Data and audio stay on your machine (§12).

## 8. Acceptance criteria (definition of done)

On **test**, with the final run and its tuned guidance weight:

| # | Criterion | Bar |
|---|-----------|-----|
| 1 | Hit timing (§7.2.1) | Median F above the baseline's median `timing_f` on the same test songs and labels, overall and in ≥ 4 of 5 labels |
| 2 | Physical plausibility (§7.2.2) | p50 and p95 of every listed quantity within ±15 % of real |
| 3 | Hits agree with motion (§7.2.3) | Generated rate ≥ real rate − 5 percentage points |
| 4 | Control (§7.2.4) | Spearman(requested NPS, generated hit rate) ≥ 0.9; median \|generated hit rate / real `hps` − 1\| ≤ 0.15; with NPS dropped, median hit rate strictly increasing Easy → Expert+ |
| 5 | Diversity (§7.2.5) | Sample-to-sample distance ≥ 50 % of real player-to-player distance |
| 6 | Seams (§7.2.6) | ≥ 99 % of joins within the real p99 |
| 7 | Visual check (§7.3) | 6 test songs picked across tempo and genre, each a generated + real pair; you judge ≥ 5 of the 6 generated clips plausible |
| 8 | Coverage | Motion tracks for ≥ 99 % of accepted replays, or a recorded reason |
| 9 | No leakage | `stats.json` from train only; checkpoints and `w` chosen on val; test evaluated once (automated checks on the run log) |
| 10 | Resume | Straight vs. resumed training gives identical weights (test) |
| 11 | Speed | `generate()` ≤ 60 s for a 3-minute song at one difficulty |
| 12 | Report | All §7.2 metrics per label, val and test, next to real replays and the baseline; ablation table; timings |

Bars 1–6 are fixed before any run. They change only by your approval,
logged in §14.

CLI: `kata motion prepare | train | sample | evaluate | viewer | report`.
Torch comes from the existing `audio` extra; **no new dependencies**. DDIM
is written in-house (~100 lines) rather than pulling in `diffusers`.
three.js is loaded by the viewer page from a CDN at a pinned version.

## 13. Risks

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|--------|------------|
| Overfitting to ~2.5k train songs (most likely) | Good val loss early, then worse motion on new songs | Dropout, EMA, checkpoints chosen on val metrics, song-first sampling, stems ablation. If still bad: add Tier B replays (D6) |
| Averaged, limp swings | Fails criteria 2 and 3 | x₀ prediction with tip and tip-velocity losses; viewer from the pilot run on |
| A frame convention is wrong (offsets, blade axis, mirroring, height) | Systematically wrong motion | Every one is checked in the pilot, before the fetch (§10) |
| Hits drift from the music at long range | Timing F drops late in songs | Audio cross-attention per window; criterion 1 per song section in the report |
| Guidance trades plausibility for control | Criterion 4 passes, criterion 2 fails | `w` chosen subject to both (§7.1) |
| Windows `DataLoader` + memory maps slow or fragile | Throughput far below estimate | Verified at setup; fallbacks in §6.6 |
| Test leakage | Optimistic acceptance numbers | Test evaluated once; automated check (criterion 9) |
| Hand y depends on player height | Generated hands at the wrong height for some players | Pilot regression (§3.2) decides normalisation |
